AI Engineering Path | History of AI — Dartmouth 1956

If AI has a birthday, it is summer 1956 at Dartmouth College.

John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Claude Shannon, and others met to study how machines could simulate learning and intelligence. In that meeting, the term “Artificial Intelligence” became the common name for the field.

The claim was bold: every part of intelligence could, in principle, be described so clearly that a machine could copy it.

That belief created energy. It also created promises that early computers could not keep.

Takeaway:
A field needs a clear name and a shared goal. It also needs honest limits. Dartmouth gave AI its name. Later winters forced the limits into the open.
