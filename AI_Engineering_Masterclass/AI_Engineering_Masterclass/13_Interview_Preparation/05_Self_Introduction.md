# Self Introduction — Apne Aap Ko Confidently Present Karo

> *"'Tell me about yourself' — yeh sabse simple lagta hai. Sabse loge galat jawab dete hain. Ya toh resume padh lete hain. Ya 10 minute baat karte hain. Ya itna nervous hote hain ki structure kho dete hain. Yaar, yeh conversation ka first impression hai. Is file mein main tujhe ek framework dunga — jo mujhe personally pasand hai — jisse tera introduction sharp, confident, aur memorable ho. Aur main tujhe actual scripts dunga alag backgrounds ke liye."*

---

## Why Self Introduction Matters

**It's the first thing interviewers hear.**
Sets tone for entire interview.
Gives them context for your answers.
Shows communication skills.

**What interviewers are evaluating:**
Can you communicate clearly?
Do you know what's important about your own background?
Are you confident without being arrogant?
Is there a coherent story here?

**Common mistakes:**
Reading the resume out loud (they already have it).
Too long (> 3 minutes).
Too short ("I'm Rahul, I do ML").
No narrative thread — just list of jobs.
Saying "basically," "you know," "like" excessively.

---

## The Framework — PAST → PRESENT → FUTURE

**PAST:** Where did you come from? What have you done?
**PRESENT:** What are you doing now? What are your strengths?
**FUTURE:** Why this role? Why now?

**Time allocation:**
PAST: 30-40% of introduction (30-60 seconds)
PRESENT: 30-40% (30-60 seconds)
FUTURE: 20-30% (15-30 seconds)

**Total: 90-120 seconds. Maximum 2 minutes.**

**Transition phrases:**
PAST → PRESENT: "Building on that experience..."
PRESENT → FUTURE: "Which is why I'm excited about this role..."

## Template Scripts

### Script 1 — Fresh Graduate / No Experience

**Context:** Engineering/CS graduate. Some projects. No full-time AI experience.

---
"I completed my B.Tech in Computer Science from [College] last year, where I developed a strong foundation in machine learning — I took courses in deep learning and NLP, and completed my capstone project building a sentiment analysis system for Hindi-English code-mixed text, which involved some interesting challenges around multilingual tokenization.

Outside coursework, I've been teaching myself LLM engineering. I've built two projects: a RAG-based document Q&A system using LangChain and Pinecone, which I deployed on AWS; and a fine-tuned classification model for customer intent detection.

I'm looking for a role where I can apply this foundation to real production systems — learning from experienced engineers and shipping AI features that actually users use. Your team's focus on [specific thing about company] is exactly the kind of work I want to grow into."

---

**What works:**
Specific project names.
Specific technologies (LangChain, Pinecone, AWS).
Honest about learning stage ("teaching myself," "looking to grow").
Clear future direction.

---

### Script 2 — 1-2 Years Software Experience, Transitioning to AI

**Context:** Has software engineering experience. Now moving into AI engineering.

---
"I've been a software engineer for about 2 years at [Company], primarily working on backend systems — designing APIs, working with databases, and handling production incidents. That gave me solid engineering fundamentals: I know how to write reliable code, design for scale, and work in a production environment.

Over the last year, I've been deliberately moving toward AI engineering. I've completed several deep learning courses and started working on AI projects: I built an internal RAG system at [Company] that's now used by our support team to quickly find relevant policies, reducing lookup time by 60%. I've also been self-studying LLM architecture deeply — I can talk about transformer internals, fine-tuning techniques, and evaluation.

I'm looking for a role that's specifically AI-focused, where I can bring my production engineering discipline to AI systems — someone who can both understand the ML side and make sure it runs reliably in production."

---

**What works:**
Bridges past engineering experience to AI.
Specific project with real impact (60% time reduction).
Honest about the transition.
Frames existing skills (production discipline) as asset.

---

### Script 3 — 1+ Year ML/AI Experience

**Context:** Has direct AI/ML experience. Data science, ML engineering, or research.

---
"I've been working as an ML engineer for about a year and a half at [Company], where I've been responsible for our recommendation system and recently led our move to LLM-based features. Specifically, I built a semantic search system over our product catalog that improved click-through rates by 23%, and I just finished deploying a customer-facing chat assistant that now handles 40% of our support volume.

My technical focus has been on the full LLM stack: RAG architecture, evaluation frameworks, production deployment with monitoring. I've also gotten deeply into the theory — I've read through the key papers on RLHF, Constitutional AI, and I'm currently going through Anthropic's interpretability work.

I'm at a point in my career where I want to be working on more challenging problems and alongside stronger engineers and researchers. That's what draws me to [Company] — specifically [something concrete about them]."

---

**What works:**
Concrete metrics (23% CTR improvement, 40% support volume).
Specific technical breadth (RAG, evaluation, deployment, monitoring).
Shows ongoing learning (reading papers).
Clear "why now" — wants more challenge.

---

### Script 4 — For Anthropic Specifically

**Context:** Applying to Anthropic. Safety/alignment-aware.

---
"I've been a machine learning engineer for two years, primarily working on production LLM systems. At [Company], I built our RAG-based knowledge retrieval system and a fine-tuned classification model for sensitive content detection.

What distinguishes my work from typical LLM engineering is my focus on reliability and safety. I've built systematic evaluation frameworks, implemented faithfulness monitoring for our RAG system, and thought carefully about failure modes in deployed AI. I've been following Anthropic's research closely — the Constitutional AI paper particularly influenced how I think about alignment, and the interpretability work on sparse autoencoders genuinely fascinates me.

Anthropic is specifically where I want to be because I want to work on AI that's both capable and safe — and I want to contribute to the research that makes that possible. The problems your team is working on — scalable oversight, interpretability, alignment — are the ones I think matter most."

---

**What works:**
Connects past experience to safety/alignment (content detection).
Names specific Anthropic research papers.
"Why Anthropic" is genuine and specific.
Shows awareness of what Anthropic does, not just that they exist.

## Industry-Specific Variations

### For Data Science Role (Not ML Engineering)

"My background is in [analytics/statistics]. Over the past year, I've been adding AI capabilities to my work — I've built predictive models for [specific outcome] and recently worked with LLMs for [specific use]. I'm looking to move from ad-hoc analysis to building AI systems that scale."

### For Research Role

"My background is in [domain] research. My thesis was on [topic], which involved [technical challenge]. I've published [n papers] including [key paper]. I'm most interested in [specific research direction] and believe [specific hypothesis]. I'm looking to work in an environment where I can pursue that research direction with real resources and strong collaborators."

### For Startup

"I've been working at [Company], which is a smaller startup. I'm used to owning features end-to-end — I've done everything from data pipeline to model training to API deployment to monitoring. I move fast and ship things. I'm looking for somewhere I can continue that level of ownership while working on a more ambitious mission than where I am now."

## What to Do After Your Introduction

**Hand it back:**
"That's the overview. Happy to go deeper on any of that."

**Watch for cues:**
Interviewer looks interested in X → Go deeper.
Interviewer moves on → They have what they need.

**Don't:**
Add more and more details until they interrupt.
Ask "Is that good?" (it sounds insecure).
Start over if you stumble. Just continue.

---

## Common Mistakes and How to Fix Them

**Mistake 1: Too long**
If you're still talking at 3 minutes: Too long.
Fix: Practice with timer. Cut to essentials.

**Mistake 2: Reading your resume**
"I graduated in 2021, then I worked at Company A for 2 years, then Company B for 1 year..."
Fix: Tell a story, not a timeline.

**Mistake 3: Vague impact**
"I improved the recommendation system."
Fix: "I improved recommendation click-through rates by 15%."

**Mistake 4: No "why this company"**
"So that's my background."
Fix: End with clear connection to this specific company/role.

**Mistake 5: Technical overload in intro**
10 minutes of technical detail in the introduction.
Fix: Save technical depth for later. Intro is just the map.

**Mistake 6: Apologizing for your background**
"I know I don't have much experience but..."
"I'm just a fresher but..."
Fix: Lead with what you DO have. Confidence, not apology.

## Practicing Your Introduction

**How to practice:**

**Step 1 — Write it:**
Draft your introduction using the framework.
300-350 words for 90-120 seconds.

**Step 2 — Record yourself:**
Phone recording.
Watch back: Pacing? Fillers? Eye contact?

**Step 3 — Practice timing:**
Set timer for 2 minutes.
Practice until you can do it naturally in under 2 minutes.

**Step 4 — Practice variation:**
Technical interviewer → More technical details.
HR interviewer → More narrative, less technical.
"Tell me about yourself" → Use the full framework.
"Walk me through your resume" → Chronological but still narrative.

**Step 5 — Practice live:**
With a friend. With a mirror. In mock interviews.
The first 10 live deliveries: Awkward. That's normal. Push through.

## Answering "Why Are You Looking?"

**If currently employed:**
"I've had a great run at [Company]. I've learned a lot, particularly about [X]. I'm looking now because I want to work on [harder problems / bigger scale / specific technology]. When [this role / your company] came up, it was a clear fit."

**If between jobs:**
"I recently finished at [Company] — the role was focused on [X], and I'm now looking for my next challenge. I've been using this time to deepen my AI skills, specifically [what you've been learning]."

**If transitioning:**
"I've been in [current field] for [n] years. Over the last year, I've been deliberately moving toward AI engineering — I've done [specific learning/projects]. I'm at the point where I'm ready for a full-time AI engineering role."

**Avoid:**
Badmouthing previous employer.
"I need more money."
"My current company is boring."
(Even if true — these are red flags.)

## Sample Answers to Common Follow-Up Questions

**"What are your strengths?"**
"My strongest areas are: [one technical strength with evidence], [one execution/delivery strength with evidence], and [one collaboration/communication strength with evidence]. For example, [brief story for one of them]."

**"What is your weakness?"**
Pick real but not disqualifying. Show self-awareness + action.
"I tend to want to understand things very deeply before acting, which occasionally means I move slower than ideal. I've been working on this by setting explicit timeboxes — I'll spend X hours investigating before making a decision even with incomplete information."

**"Where do you want to be in 5 years?"**
"Technically, I want to be expert-level in [specific area]. In terms of impact, I want to have shipped AI systems that [specific meaningful impact]. At [this company], I see a path to that through [specific team/work you'd be doing here]."

## Final Interview Day Tips

**Before the interview:**
Review your introduction one more time.
Review company one-pager you prepared.
Deep breath. You've prepared.

**During introduction:**
Speak at 70% of your natural speed. Slower than you think.
Make eye contact with camera (for video).
Pause between sections. Silence is fine.
If you stumble: "Let me rephrase that." Continue.

**After introduction:**
"Happy to go deeper on any of that."
Then: Listen carefully to their next question.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Framework**: PAST (what you've done) → PRESENT (who you are now) → FUTURE (why this role)
- **Length**: 90-120 seconds maximum; interviewers have short attention spans
- **Specifics beat generics**: "23% CTR improvement" beats "improved recommendation system"
- **End with "why this company"**: Always connect to why THIS specific role/company
- **No apologies**: Lead with what you have, not what you lack
- **Practice with timer and recording**: Until it feels natural, not rehearsed
- **Variations by context**: Technical vs. HR vs. startup vs. research all get different emphasis
- **Anthropic specifically**: Name specific papers/research you follow; show genuine mission alignment
